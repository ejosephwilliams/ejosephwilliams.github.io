APHA 2026 poster · Quantitative study

Employment precarity as a social determinant of working mothers’ mental health

← All posters

Authors

  • Elizabeth Joseph-Williams
  • Ping Ma
  • Department of Health Behavior, Texas A&M University School of Public Health, College Station, TX 77843

Presenting author: Elizabeth Joseph-Williams

This page is a screen-reader-friendly text version of the poster.

Main finding

Working mothers in highly precarious jobs had much higher odds of depression and anxiety than working mothers in none/low-precarity jobs. The high-precarity group was small (n = 23), so the estimates for that group are imprecise.

Background

Women, particularly mothers of young children, are disproportionately affected by precarious employment, with clear links to adverse health outcomes. This study examines the association between precarious employment and mental health among working mothers with young children.

Methods

  • Online cross-sectional survey of a Qualtrics panel of 530 Black and White working mothers of young children, conducted from October to November 2025.
  • Outcomes: depression (PHQ-9) and anxiety (GAD-7), each defined as a score of 10 or above.
  • Exposure: degree of employment precarity, measured with the Employment Precariousness Scale (EPRES) and grouped as none/low, moderate or high/very high.
  • Multivariable logistic regression models adjusted for race, age, education, household income, family size, marital status, geographic region and number of children under five.

Results

Among respondents, 61.70% met the threshold for depression and 51.51% for anxiety.

All odds ratios shown are statistically significant (p < 0.05). Group counts are based on all 530 mothers; the adjusted models include 529 because one respondent had no income value. Non-Hispanic Black women had higher odds of depression (aOR 1.91) and anxiety (aOR 1.94) than non-Hispanic White women. Interaction terms between employment precarity and race were not statistically significant, suggesting that race did not significantly moderate the relationship.

Conclusions

Employment precarity was associated with depression and anxiety among both Black and White working mothers, reinforcing the need to address it as a social determinant of mental health.

Policy and public health efforts to improve job stability and working conditions for working mothers may help reduce depression and anxiety in this population.

PHQ-9 = Patient Health Questionnaire-9. GAD-7 = Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7. Poster presented at the APHA Annual Meeting, San Antonio, TX, November 2026.
