Research

Work, communication and the health of mothers

Dissertation

Employment quality as a social determinant of mental health among working mothers

A primary quantitative study of how precarious employment, including job insecurity and insecurity with working conditions, relates to depression and anxiety among working mothers. I developed the survey instrument, data dictionary and codebook, using common data elements for employment quality and maternal mental health, and analyze the data with multivariable regression.

Research areas

  • Employment quality and maternal mental health

    Occupational social determinants of health, precarious employment, and postpartum and perinatal mental health.

  • Preconception and perinatal health

    Timing and framing of preconception health messages; pregnancy intention and later behaviors and birth outcomes.

  • Health communication and media

    Family health communication and the role of media in Black maternal health, using qualitative methods.

  • Data harmonization

    Common data elements, and standardizing variables across national datasets such as PRAMS and BRFSS before analysis.

Methods

  • Multivariable regression, weighted survey analysis and multilevel modeling in Stata, SAS and R.
  • Qualitative interviews and focus groups, analyzed in Dedoose and NVivo.
  • Systematic review, survey design and REDCap data management.
  • Community-based participatory research and monitoring and evaluation.
