About

I am a DrPH candidate in the Department of Health Behavior at the Texas A&M University School of Public Health, and an instructor of record for an upper-division allied health seminar. My research looks at how work, social conditions and communication shape the health of mothers and women of childbearing age.

Before my doctoral studies I spent six years in Nigeria coordinating healthcare-worker training and monitoring and evaluation for maternal health programs, and I worked in NHS stop-smoking services in the UK.

Research focus

  • Employment quality and maternal mental health

    How precarious work relates to depression and anxiety among working mothers, and whether social support buffers it.

  • Preconception and perinatal health

    What young women want from preconception health education, and how behavior before and during pregnancy relates to birth outcomes.

  • Health communication and media

    How family conversations and media shape health knowledge, especially for Black maternal health.

  • Data harmonization for women’s health

    Common data elements and variable harmonization across national surveillance datasets.
